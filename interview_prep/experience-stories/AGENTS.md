# Experience Stories
Practise recounting real work with enough evidence to show judgement, ownership, and learning. Senior and staff loops use these stories to test how you act when the outcome is uncertain.

## Rubric dimensions
### context_and_stakes
Measures whether the situation, constraints, and consequences are concrete.
**2 (solid):** Sets a specific problem, actors, deadline or constraint, and why the outcome mattered.
**3 (staff):** Frames an ambiguous cross-team problem in customer, business, cost, or risk terms.
### personal_ownership
Measures the candidate's distinct decisions, work, and accountability.
**2 (solid):** Uses “I” to identify a hard project or subsystem personally owned and peer influence exercised.
**3 (staff):** Takes accountability across teams without authority and creates durable ownership beyond themselves.
### judgment_and_alternatives
Measures decision criteria, rejected options, and acknowledged sacrifice.
**2 (solid):** Compares credible alternatives and explains the chosen path against constraints.
**3 (staff):** Reframes the decision for multiple stakeholders and leaves a reusable decision mechanism.
### execution_and_risk
Measures sequencing, delivery, risk control, and response to setbacks.
**2 (solid):** Explains concrete actions, dependencies, risks, and how delivery was controlled.
**3 (staff):** Orchestrates cross-team sequencing and reduces repeat exposure through durable safeguards.
### measurable_result
Measures outcome evidence and its practical effect.
**2 (solid):** Gives a credible quantitative outcome tied to users, cost, reliability, or delivery.
**3 (staff):** Shows sustained multi-team or business impact rather than a one-off local metric.
### reflection
Measures an honest counterfactual and changed future behaviour.
**2 (solid):** Names a real mistake or earlier action they would take next time.
**3 (staff):** Converts the lesson into a practice that prevents the failure mode for others.

## Hard gates
- No distinguishable personal contribution, such as only “we decided”, caps overall at 1.0.
- No counterfactual or “I wouldn't change anything” caps `reflection` at 0.
- An unquantified result caps `measurable_result` at 1.

## Common failure modes
- “We” throughout, leaving the candidate's contribution untestable.
- A deadline called “tight” with no constraint or consequence.
- The chosen action presented as obviously correct with nothing sacrificed.
- Conflict resolved by “I explained I was right”.
- Ending with “I learned communication is important”.
- Offering a cosmetic weakness rather than a credible mistake.

## Strong vs weak
- **Strong:** "I proposed the RFC, ran the load test and persuaded the platform team."
  **Weak:** "We decided to migrate."
- **Strong:** "p99 fell 1.2s → 180ms, cutting checkout abandonment 1.4%; the playbook was adopted by six teams."
  **Weak:** "Performance improved and the project was successful."
- **Strong:** "If I repeated it I'd build the compatibility harness before starting migration talks — three teams lost time waiting."
  **Weak:** "I wouldn't change anything."
- **Strong:** "I rejected the rewrite because it missed the audit deadline; we isolated the hot path first."
  **Weak:** "The rewrite was clearly best."
