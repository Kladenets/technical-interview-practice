A recent feature I made... I think I can talk about our geolocation improvements in the Navigation team, which I sort of inherited and is a larger initiative, but I can focus on the specific parts I've completed so far. Basically, we support a number of markets on ikea.com, and our header fragment owns the geolocation code, for which we have a couple of providers. Once we moved to cloudflare, we wanted to explore using cloudflare's IP geolocation instead since it is essentially free with every request, great (in theory). Before beginning work, I think it's important to establish requirements for the solution and what "success" looks like. In this case, one of our goals was to reduce the geolocation spend, which we could do easily by not geolocating at all anymore - problem solved? Obviously not - we have to balance our desire to reduce cost with our other priorities, which in this case would be geolocation accuracy for an improved user experience, which translates to increased conversion rates and higher "happy customer scores". So I reached out to our data and analytics team to see if they could provide me with measurable data points that we can use to help us determine if cloudflare geolocation would be worth switching to (per market). Navigation is upper funnel, so it's a bit tricky to link the impact of our solution directly to conversion at the end of the day, but I wanted to see what they could come up with anyway, so we could look at this in terms of customer behavior and dollars. Like what is the impact on conversion of a user having to manually change their zip code from the geolocated one, what is the impact of miscommunicated availability (customer corrects their zip code and availability is different for what they were buying), etc. This was a step backward from the approach of the devs I took over for, who had planned to migrate everyone to cloudflare all at once, no conversion tracking, no geolocation quality data, which I pushed back pretty hard on. First thing I did was improve the reliability and observability of our inherited solution - most of the geolocation errors weren't being logged to sentry, and the ones that were used to include protected information like postal codes, which I removed. We didn't know if we were failing to load the SDK, if geolocation itself was failing, or if it was failing to resolve a postal code, nothing. Also, we were duplicating calls to the SDK and driving up our bill, using Google to reverse geocode data we'd already geocoded earlier. Next thing I did was determine how we were going to evaluate cloudflare's solution. I decided that since it was basically free, we could tweak our implementation to always consume the cloudflare geolocation data, and compare it to what we were paying for from either google or IPAPI, whichever is configured in the market (mostly google). When we have consent from the user for IP geolocation, we call the configured provider, and then calculate the delta between the lat/long coordinates returned from it and what we get from cloudflare, sending that to our analytics along with if the postal code matched. Additionally, if the user approves browser GPS access, we do the same calculations for both providers, saving the deltas against the GPS lat/long, which is always the most accurate. This way we can get a pretty clear picture of how cloudflare compares to the already working solution per market, as well as how accurate both are compared to GPS data, and we don't have to disrupt the market with an AB test which could negatively impact business for weeks depending on how cloudflare performs in that market. That code has yet to go out, since I've been tackling some other tech debt we inherited, as well as leading the transition of our team's code to a monorepo, as our leadership has decided that all teams need to move to that method.
