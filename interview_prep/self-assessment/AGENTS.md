# Self-assessment
This category practises honest, evidence-led accounts of strengths, gaps, and direction. It matters because polished narratives are easy to rehearse while mature self-knowledge predicts growth and fit.

## Rubric dimensions
### specificity
Measures whether claims are concrete, falsifiable, and bounded.
**2 (solid):** States a clear strength or gap with observable behaviour.
**3 (staff):** Connects specific behaviour to broader systems and people effects.

### self_awareness
Measures whether the candidate names a genuine limitation rather than a safe humblebrag.
**2 (solid):** Names a real weakness and the consequence it caused.
**3 (staff):** Understands its systemic effects and deliberately designs around it.

### evidence
Measures whether each significant claim has a named supporting situation.
**2 (solid):** Backs strengths and gaps with concrete context, action, and outcome.
**3 (staff):** Uses evidence across roles and distinguishes personal ownership from team results.

### active_remediation
Measures whether improvement work is concrete and measurable.
**2 (solid):** Explains a current practice and feedback signal for a real weakness.
**3 (staff):** Has restructured work, roles, or feedback loops to sustain improvement.

### narrative_coherence
Measures whether career choices form a deliberate, credible direction.
**2 (solid):** Links moves to lessons and a clear next step.
**3 (staff):** Articulates intentional trade-offs in scope, craft, and leadership trajectory.

### fit_reasoning
Measures why this role and level fit their next development step.
**2 (solid):** Connects role needs to concrete experience and desired growth.
**3 (staff):** Explains the leverage they seek and what they deliberately will not optimise for.

## Hard gates
- A humblebrag weakness caps `self_awareness` at 0 and overall at 1.0.
- Strengths with no situational evidence cap `evidence` at 1.
- A weakness with no consequence caps `self_awareness` at 1.

## Common failure modes
- Using adjectives with no situations.
- Choosing a weakness because it is safe rather than true.
- Naming no cost the weakness has caused.
- Offering remediation with no feedback signal.
- Reading a résumé aloud instead of telling a narrative.
- Giving fit reasoning that applies to any role.
- Over-disclosing a disqualifying gap without mitigation.

## Strong vs weak
- **Strong:** "I under-communicate in long solo pushes; my manager learned about a slipped ledger rewrite in week three, so I now post Friday status updates."
  **Weak:** "My weakness is that I am a perfectionist."
- **Strong:** "My strength is reducing ambiguous incidents to decisions; during the billing outage I wrote the timeline that let three teams agree on the rollback."
  **Weak:** "I am a strong communicator."
- **Strong:** "I moved from feature delivery into platform work after seeing the same integration delay recur across three launches."
  **Weak:** "I wanted a new challenge."
- **Strong:** "I ask my lead quarterly whether a surprise reached them late; fewer surprises is the signal my update habit works."
  **Weak:** "I am working on it."
- **Strong:** "I am choosing not to be the deepest specialist in every subsystem so I can build durable technical direction across teams."
  **Weak:** "I want to keep growing."
